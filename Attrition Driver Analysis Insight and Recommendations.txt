1) Employee Attrition by Job Role
Insight

Laboratory Technicians, Sales Executives, and Research Scientists account for a large proportion of employee attrition, while Managers experience relatively low turnover.

Recommendation
Conduct role-specific retention reviews for high-turnover positions.
Improve career progression, mentoring, and recognition for these roles.
Review workload and compensation to identify factors contributing to turnover.

2) Employee Attrition by Job Satisfaction
Insight

Employees with Low and Medium job satisfaction have noticeably higher attrition than those with High or Very High job satisfaction.

Recommendation
Conduct regular employee engagement surveys.
Strengthen recognition and reward programs.
Train managers to provide effective feedback and career support.
Create clear career development opportunities to improve employee satisfaction.

3) Employee Attrition by Overtime Status
Insight

A significant proportion of employees who leave the organization work overtime, suggesting that excessive workload may contribute to employee turnover.

Recommendation
Monitor overtime hours across departments.
Balance workloads and recruit additional staff where necessary.
Promote work-life balance through flexible work arrangements and wellness initiatives.

4) Employee Attrition by Work-Life Balance
Insight

Employees reporting Low or Very Low work-life balance experience higher attrition than those with Good or Very Good work-life balance.

Recommendation
Introduce flexible working arrangements where feasible.
Encourage managers to support healthy workloads and leave utilization.
Expand employee wellbeing and mental health initiatives.

5) Employee Attrition by Business Travel
Insight

Most employees travel Rarely, followed by Travel Frequently, while Non-Travel employees make up the smallest group. To determine whether travel is a true driver of attrition, compare the attrition rate within each travel category, not just the number of employees.

Recommendation
Review travel policies to ensure frequent travellers receive adequate support.
Consider virtual meetings when appropriate.
Monitor whether frequent travel is linked to lower engagement or higher turnover
